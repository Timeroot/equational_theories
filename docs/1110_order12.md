# E1110 has no model of order twelve

`Spectrum.not_order_1110_12` is proved in Lean in
[`OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean).
Its 35 compact certificates total **1,693,604 bytes (1.62 MiB)**, down from
6,732,776 bytes. The largest is now 131,028 bytes. The five optimized branches
each check in **4.9–5.7 seconds** in a normal Lean build; the old largest branch
took 145 seconds. Rebuilding the final three changed certificates together
with the exclusion and separation theorems took 21 seconds, with other
dependencies cached. No `sorry` is used.

This decides a previously open spectrum inclusion: E167 has a model of order
12, whereas E1110 does not. Thus `Spec(E167)` is not contained in `Spec(E1110)`;
in particular E1110 is not finitely FO-definable from E167 on the same carrier.
Both conclusions are in
[`OrderTwelve/Separation.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/OrderTwelve/Separation.lean).

## Why the squaring permutation helps

Write E1110 as `y*((y*(x*x))*y)=x`. In a finite model the left translations,
right translations, and squaring map `D(x)=x*x` are all bijective. Moreover D
has no nontrivial two-cycle. If `a*a=b` and `b*b=a`, the law at `(a,b)` gives
`b*(a*b)=a=b*b`. Left cancellation gives `a*b=b=a*a`, and another cancellation
gives `b=a`. This short argument is formalized in
[`Diagonal.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/Diagonal.lean).

Mathlib's permutation conjugacy theorem lets us relabel D to a canonical
representative of its cycle type. There are 77 partitions of twelve, of which
42 contain a two-cycle and are disposed of by the preceding lemma. The finite
representative list, inverse tables, and coverage are checked in Lean in
[`CycleRepresentatives.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/CycleRepresentatives.lean).
For the 34 nonidentity remaining permutations, fixing the entire diagonal
makes the Latin-square constraints much more effective. For the identity
diagonal (all points idempotent), we instead normalize a left translation.
Five other branches admit simultaneous diagonal and row normalization, as
described below. Every case refutes in less than a second with these encodings.
This matters: an unrestricted fifteen-minute search had returned no verdict.

The additional compression uses the established finite consequence E8,
`x*(x*x)=x`. When D starts with `D(0)=1`, the first two entries of row zero
are therefore `0*0=1` and `0*1=0`. Suppose D fixes every label from k onward.
We can relabel just those fixed points, leaving the first k labels unchanged,
so that `0*y ≤ y+k−2`. This relabeling commutes with D and hence preserves the
chosen diagonal. The construction is an elementary induction: whenever the
next row entry exceeds the bound, swap that output label with the next allowed
one. Earlier normalized entries stay fixed. This is formalized in
[`RowNormalization.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/RowNormalization.lean),
and the E8 and row-bound clauses are justified in
[`ForwardEncoding.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/ForwardEncoding.lean).
We use it for cycle types 3, 4, 5, 3+3, and 4+4, with all other points fixed.
Their certificates are respectively 9,824, 47,056, 6,444, 62,420, and 99,320
bytes. In particular, the four-cycle branch shrank from 3,153,148 bytes to
47,056 bytes; its solver run now takes 0.22 seconds.

The encoding and its soundness proof are shared with the existing small-order
chain-law exclusions. Most branches fix the diagonal, the idempotent branch
uses the usual first-row normalization, and the five optimized branches use
both normalizations together. CaDiCaL and the compact-data converter
supply data only. Lean reconstructs propagation hints and checks them using
`Std.Tactic.BVDecide.LRAT.check_sound`; the native checks are registered with
`@[spectrum_native]` and the public theorem is audited with
`spectrum_assert ... complete`.

There is also a useful general subclass fact, proved without finiteness:
**commutative E1110 magmas are idempotent**. Under commutativity the law reads
`L_y^3 D=id`. Two overlapping instances give `x*(x*x)=x*x`; substituting this
back into the original identity gives `x*x=x`. See
[`Commutative.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/Commutative.lean).

## Reproduction

```sh
python3 scripts/spectrum_1110_twelve.py
lake build equational_theories.Spectrum.Equation1110.OrderTwelve.Separation
lake env lean --run equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/OrderTwelve/Export.lean /tmp/e1110-twelve-cnf
python3 scripts/spectrum_1110_twelve.py --check-lean-cnf /tmp/e1110-twelve-cnf
```

The Python generator independently reproduces the exact Lean CNFs and checks
the saved hashes, case coverage, and generated certificate modules. `--solve`
recreates the compact data with bounded solver runs; ordinary builds never
invoke a model finder. Data and per-case timings are in
[`1110_order12.json`](../data/spectrum/1110_order12.json).
To regenerate just the five optimized branches, use
`python3 scripts/spectrum_1110_twelve.py --solve --only 2 4 6 9 20`.
