# E1483 has no model of order 12

`Spectrum.not_order_1483_12`, in
[`Exclusion12.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1483/Exclusion12.lean),
is a complete Lean proof. Its finite combinatorial reductions and all certificate
checks have passed `spectrum_assert ... complete`; there are no `sorry` obligations.
This excludes one more order, without assuming a proposed general description of
E1483's spectrum.

## Mathematical reduction

Write `r(a)` for the number of distinct entries in row `a`. The E1483 law implies
its dual, regularity of the left and right translations, and equality of row and
column ranks. An edge `a → b` means that `b` occurs in row `a`. Along any edge,
`x ↦ (a*x, x*b)` is injective, so `12 ≤ r(a)r(b)`. A constant or bijective row would
force the whole cardinality to be a power of two, which 12 is not. Consequently
the minimum row rank `k` lies between 2 and 11. Choose a minimum-rank row as row
zero, and rename its image to `{1,…,k}` or `{0,…,k−1}`, according to whether it
contains zero. The latter case is impossible for `k = 2,3`, by the edge rank bound.

Further renaming makes the first row lexicographically least on its image, while
fixing the distinguished labels zero and one. In particular, `0*i ≤ i+1`. If zero
is idempotent, its left translation has constant third iterate, and label one can
be chosen so that `0*1 = 0`. The remaining labels are sorted first by membership
in column zero and then by their entry in row zero. Regularity makes row zero
injective on that column image. Permuting equal fibers outside both images then
allows lexicographic normalization of row one, without changing row zero. The
existence of all these normal forms is proved using finite minima; no search
result is used to justify a symmetry restriction.

At minimum rank three, an adjacent row of rank four saturates the edge rank
bound. The resulting bijection with a product of translation images gives the
identities `0*(b*x) = b` and `(x*0)*b = 0`. Guarded clauses use these identities
unless the adjacent row has rank at least five. This is the coordinate argument
in `SharpCoordinates.lean`, specialized by `SharpEncoding.lean`.

The remaining cases are finite, but the partitions are chosen to keep replay
short. The rank-four case containing zero has a particularly useful mathematical
reduction: its all-zero image head permits 21 sorted first rows, which lie in
five isomorphism classes. Lean verifies the row coverage and the permutations
reducing 21 rows to five representatives. Other difficult ranks use small
partitions of the entries `0*1`, `0*2`, and occasionally `0*3`, `0*4`. Every
partition is proved either by short bounds on these entries or by a separately
checked coverage certificate.

A further consequence, proved in `NoRankFourLoop.lean`, is that if every row has
rank at least four, any vertex with a self-loop has rank at least five: otherwise
moving that vertex to zero would give one of the excluded rank-four cases
containing zero. This consequence is retained as a mathematical lemma; the final
certificate partition does not need the additional clauses explored from it.

## Size and checking time

The installed package has **68 payloads, 22,616,552 bytes in total (21.57 MiB)**.
The largest payload is **1,152,696 bytes (1.10 MiB)**. All selected native certificate checks
completed in **49 seconds or less per module** on the development machine.
These are local measurements, not hardware-independent bounds.

| Part | Payloads | Compressed bytes | Observed individual checks |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Minimum rank 2 | 1 | 733,672 | 29 s |
| Minimum rank 3, including coverage | 9 | 5,823,384 | 9–49 s |
| Minimum rank 4, zero in its image | 11 | 1,632,352 | at most 24 s |
| Minimum rank 4, zero outside its image | 30 | 11,037,632 | at most 42 s |
| Minimum rank 5, zero outside its image | 4 | 2,104,736 | 10–40 s |
| Remaining ranks | 13 | 1,284,776 | at most 26 s |

The thirty rank-four outside cases and their Lean coverage proof built in
53.01 seconds in a normal parallel `lake build`. The final public theorem built
in 6.5 seconds with its checked dependencies available. Individual processes
used roughly 3–4 GiB at the larger checks; parallel builds use more aggregate
memory. Smaller total payloads were possible with unsplit certificates, but their
individual replay times were several minutes. The selected partition favors
short independent checks with a modest increase in total storage.

Almost all the expensive replay work is reconstruction of RUP hints. For an
obsolete 2.28 MB rank-five certificate, measurements were 3.06 seconds to build
the CNF, 137.71 seconds to reconstruct hints, and 3.47 seconds for the verified
LRAT checker. That certificate has been replaced by four shorter ones. The
superseded rank-three/rank-five unsplit payloads and large early rank-four
certificates are not installed.

## Data, reproducibility, and trust

The main manifest is
[`data/spectrum/1483_order12.json`](../data/spectrum/1483_order12.json).
Rank-three partitions and rank-four row coverage have separate manifests,
referenced there. Three audit scripts reproduce the independent DIMACS formulas
and check payload hashes and generated wrappers:

```sh
python3 scripts/spectrum_1483_twelve.py
python3 scripts/spectrum_1483_twelve_rank3.py
python3 scripts/spectrum_1483_twelve_inside_four.py
lake build equational_theories.Spectrum.Equation1483.Exclusion12
```

The main script's `--export-cnf DIR` exports its formulas; `--check-lean-cnf DIR`
compares them against independent Lean exports. The specialized rank-three and
rank-four scripts audit their additional formulas. Selected formulas were also
compared against Lean exports during development, and every installed payload
has been replayed against its Lean-defined CNF.

CaDiCaL generated the external proofs. Optional backward trimming with
`scripts/spectrum_retrim_certificate.py` removed unused proof clauses before
compact encoding. The distributed files store compact RUP clauses, deletions,
and masks selecting needed input clauses, compressed with XZ. During Lean
elaboration, `CompactRup.reconstruct` finds LRAT hints; this reconstruction is
untrusted. Lean's verified `LRAT.check`, evaluated with `native_decide`, checks
those hints, and `LRAT.check_sound` connects acceptance to unsatisfiability.
The CNF soundness, normalization, and coverage theorems then connect this to the
original E1483 law. Solver termination messages and external UNSAT reports are
not used as proof axioms.
