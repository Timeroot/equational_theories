# E1110 has no thirteen-element model

11 October 2026.

The search is exhaustive: every possible multiplication table is covered by a
proved squaring-permutation reduction and one of 45 finite refutations. The
formalization is in
[`OrderThirteen/Exclusion.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/OrderThirteen/Exclusion.lean),
with public declaration `Spectrum.not_order_1110_13`.

This separates the spectra of **E167, E467, E677, E704, E1076, E1286, E1313,
E1486, and E1516** from being contained in the spectrum of E1110: each of
those equations has a model of order 13. It does not classify the full spectrum
of E1110. The separate order-12 exclusion is in
[`OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean).

## The reduction

Write `x*y` for the operation and `D(x)=x*x`. The law is

```
y*((y*D(x))*y) = x.
```

In a finite model every left translation, every right translation, and `D`
are bijections. These are the general theorems in
[`SmallOrders/Chain.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/SmallOrders/Chain.lean).
Moreover, `D` has no nontrivial two-cycle. If `a*a=b` and `b*b=a`, the law
at `(a,b)` says `b*(a*b)=a=b*b`; left cancellation gives `a*b=b=a*a`,
and a second left cancellation gives `a=b`. This short argument is
`Spectrum.E1110.square_two_cycle` in
[`Diagonal.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/Diagonal.lean).

Mathlib's theorem that the cycle type determines a permutation up to conjugacy
lets us label `D` as a canonical union of consecutive cycles. The explicit
list in
[`CycleRepresentatives.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/CycleRepresentatives.lean)
covers all 101 partitions of 13. Removing types containing a two-cycle leaves
45 cases: the identity permutation and 44 nonidentity types. The identity
case is exactly the idempotent case; its first-row normalization is proved
separately. No assumption of commutativity, associativity, or a group model
is made.

The key second reduction uses the previously proved finite implication
E1110 → E8, `x*(x*x)=x` (`Finite.Equation1110_implies_Equation8`). Every
nonidentity canonical squaring permutation starts with `D(0)=1`. Consequently
row zero begins `0*0=1`, `0*1=0`: its first two labels form a transposition.
Suppose all labels at least `m` are fixed by `D`. Relabel those fixed points
successively to obtain

```
(0*y).val ≤ y.val + (m-2).
```

At step `y≥2`, if the output is too large, swap it with label `y+m-2`.
Both labels lie outside the support of `D`, and neither occurs in an earlier
normalized row entry; hence the swap preserves the diagonal and previous
bounds. This induction, valid at every order, is
`RowNormalization.normalize_supported_row` in
[`RowNormalization.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/RowNormalization.lean).
When `D` has just a three-cycle, this is the full ordinary row-chain bound
`0*y≤y+1`; for a four-cycle it is `0*y≤y+2`. Larger supports yield the same
useful, weaker constraint.

This additional symmetry reduction made the difficult cases practical. The
three-cycle case dropped from a 13.3 MB certificate to **19,140 bytes**, and
the four-cycle case from 3.14 MB to **121,908 bytes**. It also shrank thirteen
other cycle-type certificates, including one from 1.85 MB to 123 KB.

The Boolean encoding is one-hot table data, Latin-square constraints, the
original E1110 identity, and these justified normalization conditions.
CaDiCaL supplies refutation data only. Lean reconstructs and checks the
compact RUP data with its verified LRAT checker using registered native
computation; the solver's UNSAT report is not itself a proof assumption.

## Data and reproduction

[`1110_order13.json`](../data/spectrum/1110_order13.json) records the exact
CNF and certificate hashes. The 45 compressed certificates occupy
**5,100,884 bytes (4.86 MiB)** altogether. The largest is the idempotent
case, at 925,848 bytes; every other certificate is below 631 KB. The
three-cycle and four-cycle native checks took approximately 7.6 and 6.6
seconds respectively, and their proof-producing searches took 0.27 and
0.52 seconds. The final assembly of all cases checked in about ten seconds.
These are timings on this workspace, not guaranteed CI timings.

All 45 CNFs can be exported independently by Lean and compared byte for byte
with the Python generator:

```sh
lake build equational_theories.Spectrum.Equation1110.OrderThirteen.Exclusion
lake env lean --run equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/OrderThirteen/Export.lean .cache/e1110-order13-export
python3 scripts/spectrum_1110_thirteen.py --check-lean-cnf .cache/e1110-order13-export
```

`scripts/spectrum_1110_order13.py` also reproduces the earlier search without
certificate generation. The squaring normalization was regression-tested at
orders 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9, where it retains the known positive models. Unrestricted
900-second runs, and several weaker normalized runs, timed out; those timeouts
were not used as exclusions. The successful decomposition typically solves
ordinary cycle types in under a few seconds. All thirteen additional
support-normalized proof-producing searches took under two seconds each.

[`ForwardEncoding.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1110/ForwardEncoding.lean)
formalizes the extra E8 unit constraints and row bounds. Attempts to prove
that squaring is an automorphism, or that the idempotents form a subquasigroup,
did not establish either assertion; neither is assumed by this proof.
