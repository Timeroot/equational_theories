# Order 339: a projective-plane frame closes the largest E667/E883 gap

**Proved in Lean, 2026-10-05.** There is an idempotent E63 model of order
339, hence models of E667 and E883 at that order. Both ordinary cofinite
tails improve from **340 to 220**. E667 now has 16 unresolved orders:

```
12, 15, 24, 30, 39, 48, 51, 60, 75, 87, 96, 102, 159, 174, 195, 219.
```

E883 has 20 unresolved orders. Its same bounds transfer to E1323, E1526,
and their duals. The separate exclusions remain 3,6 for E667 and 3,6,9
for E883. This result does not resolve any of the smaller open orders.

## Construction

The projective plane PG(2,3) has thirteen points and thirteen four-point
lines, with exactly one line through each pair of distinct points. Replace
each point by a two-point hole. On the four holes belonging to each line,
put Bennett's partial eight-point C3 quasigroup (Figure 1 of *Quasigroup
Identities and Mendelsohn Designs*, 1989), taking left division to obtain
E63. This gives a partial algebra on 26 points: products within a hole are
unused. An E63 identity whose inputs lie in different holes stays in their
one eight-point line algebra, and none of its three products uses two
points of the same hole.

Multiply this partial algebra by an ordinary thirteen-point E63 algebra.
Each of its thirteen holes now has 26 points. Adjoin one common point ∞
and fill each hole together with ∞ using the idempotent 27-point cubic
algebra. All fillings agree at ∞. If two inputs belong to one enlarged
hole, the identity holds in its 27-point algebra. Otherwise every product
uses different holes, so the identity holds coordinatewise in the partial
26-point algebra and the thirteen-point factor. Squares are evaluated in
the hole fillings, making the whole result idempotent. Its order is

```
13 · 2 · 13 + 1 = 339.
```

The thirteen-point factor need not be idempotent. Explicitly it can be
`x * y = -x + 3y` over F13. The 27-point filling is the already formalized
cubic construction over F3. This distinction is what allows the construction
to use thirteen even though there is no idempotent E63 model of that order.

  `Frame.fill_law`, `fill_idem`, and `models` are symbolic proofs.

  `certificate` checks the partial law and the two hole-avoidance conditions
  on only 26 points, by kernel reduction. `models` proves the general recipe
  below, and `idem339` is its specialization.
* [Equation667883ExtendedBounds.lean](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation667883ExtendedBounds.lean)
  exports the updated `E667.ExtendedBounds.lower`, `all_large`, `cofinite`,
  and their E883 counterparts.

There is no 339-by-339 table, SAT proof, `sorry`, or native-checking assumption
in the new proof. The first two modules took approximately 5 and 29 seconds
to build locally. The full models are also independently reconstructed and
checked by [the Python verifier](../scripts/spectrum_667_projective_frame.py).
Only recipes, the small input data, and table hashes are stored in
[the manifest](../data/spectrum/e667_projective_frame.json).

## General recipe and additional gains

The same frame gives

```
ordinary E63(m) + idempotent E63(2m+1)
    ⇒ idempotent E63(26m+1).
```

Closing this under the existing construction rules adds idempotent orders
**131, 339, and 443**. They use `(m,2m+1)=(5,11),(13,27),(17,35)` respectively.
The 35-point filling is a product of the five- and seven-point idempotent
models. The general rule also provides shorter alternative constructions
for some orders already obtained by transversal designs. The ordinary E667
and E883 gains are at 339; 131 and 443 were already ordinary models.
The idempotent E63 tail remains 689, but three of its finite gaps disappear.
Their existing definability transfers are included in the regenerated catalogue.

Reproduce the independent checks and the Lean proof with:

python3 scripts/spectrum_667_extended_bounds.py --check
python3 scripts/spectrum_generate.py --check

All checks passed, including the full 4,162-job spectrum build. The axiom
audit for `certificate`, `models`, and `idem339` reports only `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The updated tail theorems retain the
previously registered native checks for the older transversal designs;
the new projective-frame construction adds none.

## What happened to the proposed 52-with-11 route?

That remains an independent open construction problem. An idempotent
52-point model with an eleven-point submodel would also give 339, by
common-hole gluing with TD(8,41). A stronger cyclic search in this pass
excluded the template with a 41-cycle and eleven fixed points (181 seconds).
The alternative 38-point cyclic template with a 37-cycle and one fixed point
was excluded in 17 seconds. These are external SAT results about restricted
automorphism templates, not Lean exclusions of either order or of arbitrary
52-with-11 extensions. The successful projective-frame proof uses neither.

The improved search is `scripts/spectrum_667_cyclic_hole.py`. It sorts the
fixed points' distinct nonzero translation shifts and normalizes one to 1.
This does not assume arbitrary relabellings preserve a chosen hole operation:
the multiplication inside an E229 subquasigroup can be replaced independently
after solving the mixed table. For a center outside the hole no product in
E229 has both inputs in the hole; for a center inside it, its translations
preserve the complement. Thus only identities wholly inside the hole change.
The search outputs and resource limits are recorded in the companion
[research manifest](../data/spectrum/e667_339_search_20261005.json).

There is also an elementary obstruction to the usual PBD approach to
52-with-11 using block sizes 5,7,8,11. At any point outside the eleven-point
hole, the eleven hole-incident blocks consume at least 44 of its 51 neighbors.
Counting the seven remaining neighbors shows that the point belongs to
exactly one eight-point block. Such a block contains seven or eight outside
points, so the 41 outside points would partition as `41=7a+8b`, impossible
for nonnegative integers a,b. This excludes that construction method, not
the sought algebra. The projective-frame method instead uses **partial**
algebras on four two-point holes, which are not ordinary filled PBD blocks.
