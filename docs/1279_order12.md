# A smaller order-12 exclusion for E1279

11 October 2026. The public theorem remains `Spectrum.not_order_1279_12`;
its proof is in
[`OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1279/OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean).

The replacement uses **1,358,908 compressed bytes** in 35 cases, down from
2,989,456 bytes in one certificate. Its largest individual certificate is
242,028 bytes. It proves the same unrestricted nonexistence result; no
commutativity, idempotence, or affine representation is assumed.

## Mathematical reduction

Write `D(x)=x*x`. E1279 says `y*((D(x)*y)*y)=x`. Finiteness makes every left
translation, every right translation, and `D` bijective. Squaring has no
nontrivial two-cycle: suppose `a*a=b` and `b*b=a`. The law at `(b,a)` reduces
to `a*(b*a)=b=a*a`, so left cancellation gives `b*a=a`. Interchanging `a`
and `b` gives `a*b=b`. The law at `(a,a)` then reduces to `b=a`.
This short argument is `Spectrum.E1279.square_two_cycle` in
[`Normalization.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1279/Normalization.lean).

Mathlib's permutation-conjugacy theorem labels `D` as one of the 77 canonical
cycle types on twelve points. The two-cycle argument removes 42 types. Of the
35 remaining types, one is the identity (the idempotent case), and 34 have
`D(0)=1`. If all labels at least `m` are fixed by `D`, these labels can be
swapped freely while retaining the diagonal. Successively normalizing row zero
gives `0*y ≤ y+(m-1)`. At step `y`, swap an oversized output with `y+m-1`;
the swap fixes the support of `D` and every earlier normalized input and
output. The idempotent case allows the ordinary bound `0*y≤y+1`.
The normalization is proved for every finite order.

## Direct encoding and checking

Knowing `D` removes the need for auxiliary variables: `(x*x)*y` is simply
`D(x)*y`. For fixed `x,y,a,b`, any two of

```
D(x)*y=a,    a*y=b,    y*b=x
```

imply the third, by the law and cancellation. Encoding these three directions
with ordinary multiplication-table atoms gives only **1,728 variables**, down
from 3,456. Each case has 90,828–90,919 clauses. The soundness proof is in
[`DirectEncoding.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1279/DirectEncoding.lean).

CaDiCaL found every direct refutation in under half a second. Lean checks the
compact RUP data using its verified LRAT checker and the repository's registered
native-computation policy. Five balanced batches of seven cases permit parallel
checking. With dependencies cached, each batch built in 21–23 seconds;
the certificate assembly took 4.4 seconds and the mathematical case assembly
11 seconds. The resulting parallel build took roughly 38 seconds, compared
with 477 seconds for the previous single certificate, on this workspace.
No solver's UNSAT report is accepted as an axiom, and the final
`spectrum_assert ... complete` rejects unregistered obligations.
