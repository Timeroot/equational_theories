the reversible first-order constructions to use left and right cubes.

## Why generation obstructs term recovery

Suppose a source operation `M` has a companion operation `N`, and a term in `N`
recovers `M`. Every subset closed under `N` is then closed under `M`. Consequently,
if a point generates `n` distinct elements under `M`, it must generate at least
`n` elements under `N`. This only needs a recovering term for the particular
model; it does not assume the same term works in every source model. An
idempotent point is the one-element special case. In fact,
`idempotent_companion_iff` proves that a magma has an idempotent term-structural
companion exactly when it is already idempotent. The new witnesses settle all
remaining catalogue arrows into E3 and E3715 for both term-structural variants.

[RecoverySubmagmas.lean](../equational_theories/Definability/RecoverySubmagmas.lean)
packages these arguments. The finite witnesses in
[SmallGeneratedWitnesses.lean](../equational_theories/Definability/SmallGeneratedWitnesses.lean)
are existing ordinary countermodels, now used to answer stronger recovery
questions:

| Source | Size | Archived ordinary countermodel | Generator |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| E621 | 4 | `Generated/All4x4Tables/Refutation554.lean` | 0 |
| E626 | 5 | `Generated/All4x4Tables/Refutation846.lean` | 0 |
| E646 | 6 | `Generated/All4x4Tables/Refutation852.lean` | 0 |

All three witnesses are non-idempotent and generated by 0. Thus none admits
term-structural recovery through an idempotent companion (E3). E646 also cannot
use an E4476 companion: its one-generated submagmas have at most three elements.

[UnaryRecoveryBounds.lean](../equational_theories/Definability/UnaryRecoveryBounds.lean)
adds nineteen finite term-structural refutations using seventeen further target
laws. For each target, it displays at most four unary terms and proves an
explicit multiplication table closing that list. These are upper bounds; we do
not claim that the listed terms must be distinct in every model.

| Bound | Target laws | Sources refuted |
|---:|---|---|
| 2 | E3715 | E621, E626, E646 |
| 2 | E434, E1687, E3587, E3600, E3716, E3718 | E646 |
| 3 | E72, E4397, E4400, E4434, E4437, E4471, E4490, E4502 | E646 |
| 4 | E4402, E4416 | E646 |

The equations generating those tables are found by finite congruence closure.
Every merge records an equality proof, and the generator discards deductions
not needed for the final multiplication table. The resulting Lean contains
ordinary equational deductions, not solver certificates. Reproduce it with:

```
python3 scripts/definability_unary_recovery.py --check
lake build equational_theories.Definability.UnaryRecoveryBounds
```

The combined table module initially built in about 14 seconds (67 seconds during
the final concurrent build and audit); all its
negative declarations have standard-axiom guards. The source tables themselves
are checked with kernel `decide`. None of these additions uses `native_decide`
or introduces a `sorry`.

## A genuinely infinite obstruction

[InfiniteGeneratedWitnesses.lean](../equational_theories/Definability/InfiniteGeneratedWitnesses.lean)
reuses the existing countable E1076 construction in
[Equation1076.lean](../equational_theories/ManuallyProved/Equation1076.lean).
Its first seed includes `0*0=1` and `0*1=2`, with 0, 1, and 2 distinct. Therefore
it is non-idempotent and generates at least three elements from 0.

This refutes unrestricted term-structural recovery through E3 and E3715. For
E3715, `x*y = (x*x)*(y*y)`, put `q=x*x`. Substituting `(x,x)`, `(x,q)`, and
`(q,x)` shows `q*q=q`, `x*q=q`, and `q*x=q`. Thus `{x,q}` is closed. If its
operation recovered the E1076 seed, it would have to contain all of 0, 1, and 2,
a contradiction.

Every finite E1076 model is idempotent, so the corresponding finite arrows are
positive. The new obstruction therefore cannot have a finite counterexample.
It is derived from an already formalized extension theorem; no new compactness
or model-existence assumption is added.

## Extending reversible output swaps

[CubeOutputSwap.lean](../equational_theories/Definability/CubeOutputSwap.lean)
protects entries `(x,x)`, `(x,x*x)`, and `(x*x,x)`. This preserves squares and
both cubes, including when the remaining entries use the opposite operation.
On the remaining entries, swap two output labels chosen from the inputs, their
squares, iterated squares, and cubes. The protected-entry condition is symmetric,
and all labels survive the transformation. Applying the corresponding swap
again therefore recovers the original operation, uniformly and without
parameters.

[CubeSwapCatalogue.lean](../equational_theories/Definability/CubeSwapCatalogue.lean)
contains ten successful target-law proofs:
E4439 → E3458, E138 → E3306, E4456 → E3458, E101 → E1223,
E4434 → E3457, E4456 → E3461, E4490 → E3461, E168 → E1629,
E1489 → E99, and E1042 → E1223. These give first-order structural
arrows, with an explicit quantifier-free construction in both directions.
Survival on a finite model bank alone is never recorded as a theorem: each
accepted recipe has a Lean proof for arbitrary magmas satisfying its source law.
These individual arrows do not by themselves assert definitional equivalence
between the entire source and target theories.

## Limits of the follow-up searches

A bounded congruence-closure search on two generators found no further closed
presentation among the 499 target representatives considered. That says nothing
about whether larger presentations exist. For E101 → E1223, a separate search
of 550 forward terms through five leaves produced seven candidate term pairs on
all models of orders two and three; the archived order-four model bank rejected
all seven. This supplies neither a positive theorem nor a general refutation.
The new first-order structural construction for that pair remains valid, and
the term-structural question remains open.

For E168 → E1629 the output swap can be omitted entirely. Write `q(x)=x*x`
and `c(x)=q(x)*x`. E168 gives `q(q(x))=x` and `c(x)*q(x)=x`.
Keep entries `(a,b)` with `a=b`, `q(a)=b`, or `a=q(b)`, and transpose every other
entry. The new square remains `q`, and its product of `q(x)` and `x` remains
`c(x)`. For the next product of `q(x)` and `c(x)`, transposing gives
`c(x)*q(x)=x`. If this entry was protected, its defining equality instead gives
`q(x)=c(x)` or `x=c(x)`, and the untransposed product is also `x`. This proves
E1629. The transformation is its own inverse because squares do not change.
