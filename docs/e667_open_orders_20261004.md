## New Lean results

**All remaining models must be nonmedial.**
[Equation667MedialAffine.lean](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation667MedialAffine.lean)
proves that every finite medial E667 algebra admits an affine presentation
over an abelian group. Normalize a row and column to obtain a loop; mediality
makes its left translations commute, giving an abelian group. The original
row and column maps, after subtracting their constants, are additive. Thus
the earlier affine obstruction applies to every medial model: if three
divides its order, nine does too. Every remaining open order is 3 or 6 modulo
nine. The more general prime-root obstruction also transfers, including
primes 31 and 47.

**A proper submagma is small when squaring is bijective.**
[Equation667SubmagmaBound.lean](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation667SubmagmaBound.lean)
proves `3m ≤ n`, strengthened to `3m < n` if an idempotent lies outside the
m-point submagma. Hence the strict bound always holds in an idempotent model.
For an outside point a, write `D(x)=x*x`, and solve `D(x_y)*y=a` for each
submagma point y. Three disjoint sets are the submagma itself, its right
translate `{x*a}`, and `{x_y*a}`. The defining law proves that the last set
also lies outside the submagma. An idempotent a belongs to none of the three
sets. This counting proof uses neither a design classification nor a finite
search. Bijectivity of squaring is an essential hypothesis in the general
statement.

**Regular group constructions are impossible at orders 2 modulo 4.**
[Equation667RegularParity.lean](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation667RegularParity.lean)
proves this for every finite group and every choice of square map. In
particular it covers open orders **30, 102, and 174**. A regular construction
has the form `x*y=x h(x^-1 y)`. Latinness requires both h and
`x ↦ x^-1 h(x)` to be permutations. For every character χ into a commutative
group, multiplying its values along these two permutations forces
`∏ χ(x)=1`. At order 2 modulo 4 the sign of the regular permutation action
is a nontrivial character with an odd number of negative values, so its
product is -1. Cauchy's theorem supplies the involution that makes this
character nontrivial. This is an elementary necessary case of the
complete-mapping obstruction, not an appeal to the general Hall–Paige theorem.

The public results all pass `spectrum_assert ... complete`. Their axiom
audits contain only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.
No new `sorry`, native check, or external proof certificate is used in these
three modules. The full Spectrum import builds with them.

## Regular automorphism groups at 20 and 24

External SAT exhausts **all regular-action E667 constructions at order 24**,
including the non-idempotent cases. It also exhausts the corresponding family
at **order 20**. Ordinary order-20 models are already known; this result only
restricts their automorphisms. It does not settle either general existence
at 24 or idempotent existence at 20.
Here regular means that exactly one element of the automorphism group sends
any given point to any other given point.

For a group G and `d=h(1)`, the exact profile identity is

```
h(t^-1 h(d h(d^-1 t))) = t^-1.
```

Fixing d leaves only three profile lookups. The one-hot encoding propagates
the three corresponding literals in all directions, using injectivity of h.
It imposes permutation constraints on h and `t^-1 h(t)`. Conjugating the group
coordinates conjugates d, so one representative of each conjugacy class
suffices. Cyclic multiplier normalization is used only for actual cyclic
groups, never for the semidirect products. There are **197 cases over the
15 groups of order 24**, and
**61 cases over the five groups of order 20**. Every case returned UNSAT.
The JSON stores each group, conjugacy partition, and elementary invariants
distinguishing the groups. Positive controls reconstruct the known idempotent
orders 7 and 21 and idempotent-free orders 5 and 8, checking every E667 instance.

The group-list coverage is elementary. At order 20, the Sylow 5-subgroup is
normal, so the group is `C5 ⋊ P` with P of order four. The three actions for
`P=C4` and two for `P=C2×C2` give five groups. At order 24, a normal Sylow
3-subgroup gives `C3 ⋊ P` for a group P of order eight: the five trivial
actions and seven nontrivial action classes give twelve groups. Otherwise
conjugation on the four Sylow 3-subgroups has image S4 or A4. The former
gives S4. In the latter case the preimage of V4 is a normal group of order
eight with a nontrivial automorphism of order three. The two possibilities
give `A4×C2` and `SL(2,3)`. These are the remaining three groups.

The algebraic regular-profile criterion was already proved in Lean in
`Equation667GroupConstructions.lean`. The finite group classification and
these **258 solver refutations have not been formalized**. In particular,
they are not registered as Lean spectrum exclusions.

Other regular-action searches target cyclic and Frobenius groups at the
open orders 39, 51, 60, 75, and 87. Their bounded searches have supplied no
new positive order. For `C13 ⋊ C3` at 39, the two conjugacy classes outside
the normal C13 were exhausted; the identity and the four nonidentity classes
inside C13 remain unresolved. The report separates fixed-square cases that were
exhausted from those that timed out. A timeout does not exclude the group.

## Idempotent order 20 and submodel extensions

For the shorter E229 parastrophe, every row fixes exactly its own point and
has no 2-cycles. The earlier split covered all 39 partitions of 19 into
cycle lengths at least three. This pass exhausts the final two patterns
containing 3-cycles:

```
1 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 7       51.9 seconds
1 + 3 + 3 + 5 + 8          140.7 seconds
```

Together with the earlier cases, this rules out 3-cycles in **every** E229
left translation at order 20, externally. There remain 17 row types, all
without 3-cycles. Adding the global no-3-cycle constraint to those cases
still leaves all 17 unresolved after 180 seconds each. Mace4's corresponding
unrestricted-row search also timed out after ten minutes. This is not a
nonexistence proof for idempotent order 20.

All idempotent E667 isotopes of two particular 20-point loops were also
excluded externally: the saved nonabelian S3 Mendelsohn loop and a Steiner
loop from the cyclic STS(19) with base triples `{0,1,4}`, `{0,2,9}`,
`{0,5,11}`. These took 27.4 and 23.6 seconds. The eight-point Boolean loop
is a positive control for the isotope encoding. Exclusion of these two
isotope classes does not exclude other 20-point quasigroups.

A seven-point idempotent submodel inside a 23-point idempotent model would
give **order 87**: use TD(5,16) on five groups, adjoin a common seven-point
hole, and fill each enlarged group by the 23-point model. Similarly a
26-point model with the same seven-point submodel, together with TD(8,19),
would give **order 159**. The new submagma inequality allows both parameter
sets. Searches fixing the affine seven-point E229 table timed out at
300 seconds for both 23 and 26. The seven-point control succeeds.

An 18-point idempotent extension of the five-point mean algebra would
similarly give 96 via TD(7,13). A direct extension search returned UNSAT in
10.8 seconds. This confirms a route already ruled out by the earlier E63
order-18 row exclusions: the five-point E229 subalgebra contains 4-cycles.
It is not a new unrestricted exclusion at 18.

The largest gap has another concrete route: an idempotent **52-point model
with an eleven-point submodel** would yield `339=8·41+11` via TD(8,41), lowering
the ordinary cutoff to 220. We searched a template with eleven fixed points
carrying the affine eleven-point E229 table and one automorphism cycle of
length 41. Orbit compression gives 4,728 SAT variables. This restricted
search timed out at 180 seconds; it does not exclude such a 52-point model.
The same encoding reconstructs the known seventeen-point idempotent model
with one fixed point and a 16-cycle. The 52-point extension remains a useful
target independent of the imposed cyclic symmetry.

## Design searches

Restrictions of Desarguesian planes were tried at orders 51, 60, 87, 159,
219, and 339. Every retained line would need an already proved idempotent
E63 filling. CP-SAT included exact point and pair counts, and in some runs
the possible local incidence histograms and a normalized large line.
The bounded runs remained unknown. Searches with an anchored line also
assume the existence of a line of that size.

A separate swap search maintained the desired point count and minimized
invalid line intersections. It came within one invalid line in two cases:

| Target | Plane | Valid line intersections | Remaining invalid line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 51 | PG(2,8) | 49 of size 7, 21 of size 5, two singletons | One of size 9 |
| 60 | PG(2,9) | 51 of size 7, 18 of size 5, 18 of size 8, three singletons | One of size 6 |

Neither is a model certificate. Idempotent fillings at 9 and 6 are unavailable
and in fact excluded; the non-idempotent E667 model at 9 cannot simply be
inserted into this idempotent gluing construction. Exact repair searches
allowing up to eight deleted and eight added points timed out at 90 seconds.
The saved point sets and invalid lines make these near-designs reproducible.

At 159, three more cyclic difference-family searches normalized the distance
53 into a block of size 5, 7, or 8. They again reached two repeated distances.
For each resulting near-family, exact enumeration excludes replacing any
three blocks while leaving the others fixed. This does not exclude other
cyclic families or noncyclic designs.

Another possible route to idempotent 96 would extend a 91-point 7-block
design by five parallel classes and fill the five new points. The particular
cyclic 2-(91,7,1) design from Sage has no such choice of five classes
**including its prescribed coset parallel class**, according to the external
exact-cover solver. This says nothing about other designs or choices that
omit that prescribed class.

## Reproduction

The regular campaigns and representative positive-search probes can be run as:

```sh
python3 scripts/spectrum_667_regular_groups.py 24 --workdir /tmp/e667-reg24
python3 scripts/spectrum_667_regular_groups.py 20 --workdir /tmp/e667-reg20
python3 scripts/spectrum_667_regular_sat.py F39 --seconds 300 --workdir /tmp/e667-f39
python3 scripts/spectrum_667_submodel_search.py 23 --seconds 300 --workdir /tmp/e667-sub7
python3 scripts/spectrum_667_plane_search.py 8 51 --point-types --seconds 120 --output /tmp/e667-plane51.json
lake build equational_theories.Spectrum
```

`spectrum_667_plane_anneal.cpp` documents its design-incidence input format.
The source of plane incidences is `spectrum_667_plane_search.plane`.
The seed point sets, group parameters, base loops, restrictions, and run
budgets are retained in the JSON and discovery logs.
