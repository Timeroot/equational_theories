# Effective spectrum tails for E1083 and E1286

The following are **computer-assisted constructive bounds**:

| Law | Every order at least | Finite interval used by the certificate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| E1083 | **246,119,111** | 246,119,111 through 280,000,000,000 |
| E1286 | **4,222,119,949** | 4,222,119,949 through 5,000,000,000,000 |

These bounds are not exact spectrum characterizations. An order below the
cutoff that this construction misses is not thereby excluded.

Both laws already have complete **Lean proofs of cofiniteness**. The numerical
bounds here have reproducible positive construction certificates and an exact
integer sieve. The general induction is also proved in Lean in
`Spectrum/Equation1083_1286/EffectiveBounds.lean`. The concrete finite
certificates and the numerical sieve counts **still await Lean checking**;
these numerical cutoffs must not be labelled as Lean-proved. No `sorry` is
introduced by this work.

## Reproduction

```sh
python3 scripts/spectrum_effective_tails.py
# Or just one law:
python3 scripts/spectrum_effective_tails.py --law 1083
# Parallel replay, with every dependency checked against earlier chunks:
python3 scripts/spectrum_effective_tails.py --jobs 4
```

The checker needs Python's standard library, a C++ compiler, approximately
4 GB of working memory, and about 750 MB of cache space for both laws. It checks
the scalar and quotient-ring identities, regenerates the construction bitmaps,
replays the finite interval certificates, and checks the exact sieve counts.
It needs no model finder, optimizer, or unproved design-existence assumption.
`--reuse-bitmaps` reuses previous bitmaps after checking their SHA-256 digests;
the finite interval and sieve checks still run.

The certificates encode algebraic coefficients and construction parameters,
not multiplication tables for billions of elements. The long finite intervals
are checked in chunks, without storing a bitmap of several trillion orders.

## Algebraic seeds and finite construction rules

Over a finite commutative ring, put `x*y = ax+by`. The required identities are

```
E1083: ab(a+b²)=1,       a+a²b²+b²=0,
E1286: ab(a²+b)=1,       a+a²b²+b²=0.
```

Both operations fix zero; they are idempotent if `a+b=1`. These are precisely
the identities in `Equation1083_1286/DesignWitnesses.lean`.

The scalar certificates use rings Z/p with p at most ten million. The extension
certificates give a monic polynomial g and coefficients a,b in (Z/p)[X]/(g).
The checker verifies the identities directly. Neither primality of p nor
irreducibility of g is needed for these ring models: the monic quotient has
exactly p^deg(g) elements.

There are 553,808 scalar and 6,898 quotient-ring recipes for E1083, and 512,341
scalar and 5,193 quotient-ring recipes for E1286. The small seeds at 8, 9, and
32 are covered by these same symbolic identities. Every positive fourth-power
order is an idempotent seed for both laws. Every positive square order is an
additional pointed seed for E1083.

The finite closure uses Cartesian products, common-point gluing, and a
transversal design with one truncated group, optionally with a common point.
For the latter two constructions, idempotent block models at k and k+1 and
TD(k+1,q) give:

* order kq+r from pointed group models at q and r, with 0≤r≤q;
* order kq+r from pointed group models at q+1 and r, with 1≤r≤q+1,
  by identifying a fixed point in all groups.

The discreteness lemmas in `Equation1083_1286/Gluing.lean` justify using arbitrary
pointed group fillings with idempotent transversal blocks. Distinct inputs in
one group evaluate inside that group; inputs in different groups evaluate
inside their unique transversal block. Identifying the fixed points preserves
the same argument. Empty holes are permitted only in the version without a
common point. Idempotent group fillings give an idempotent result.

Finite fields and products supply TD(k+1,q) whenever every maximal prime-power
factor of q is at least k. Every use of this sufficient condition is checked
by integer factorization. It is never used to rule out other designs.

## Compressed finite interval certificates

First, positive construction closure is computed through one billion. The
packed source bitmaps have these SHA-256 digests:

```
E1083 models: 7e8cc20a32bd8a93cb198bdab809b70484ac33fad1e5d7ca87371ba23afacd67
E1286 models: 068b79d9f6a8ed4991ab4baf7e0ae8f2b261b2546fc4058c3878561e59fbdef4
Idempotent:   9c6395561480ac97bebdf66b2a92d704dd0f1112eed4894daba3611239778c55
```

The two idempotent bitmaps coincide. They supply the consecutive block pairs
1008/1009, 2223/2224, and many more. Extra pairs matter: an E1286 gap at
5,039,958,984 survives the original ten-pair search but is filled by enlarging
the block list.

A certificate row specifies a chunk and a tuple (k,q,common). It constructs
all permitted orders kq+r simultaneously, by translating the appropriate
prefix of the source model bitmap. The replay checker verifies the block
models, TD condition, and full-group model before applying the translation.
The full-group model comes from the source bitmap, an explicit auxiliary
recipe, or a strictly earlier verified chunk. Auxiliary recipes themselves
use only source models. Thus the proof has no circular dependencies.
Disjoint ranges can be checked in parallel: induction on chunk number combines
the results once every chunk passes.

Finally every bit of each chunk is checked. The certificate consequently proves
the entire displayed finite interval, not merely a sample of its orders.

For E1286, the long certificate begins at 4,486,445,593. A separate finite prefix
lowers the cutoff to **4,222,119,949**. Enlarging the block list handles almost
all of this prefix; the remaining order 4,256,665,752 is the Cartesian product
23·185,072,424, with both factors already in the checked source bitmap. The
replay checks this product explicitly, then checks the entire prefix. Neither
the prime gap bound nor the long certificate needs to be recomputed to obtain
this improvement.

## A uniform gap of 22,000 for transversal-design orders

Let W=30030=2·3·5·7·11·13. Define L(t) and U(t) to be the minimum and maximum
number of integers coprime to W in any interval of t consecutive integers.
These functions are effective: the pattern is periodic modulo W. For
t=aW+b, the counts are aφ(W) plus the counts in the W possible cyclic windows
of length b. Checking those windows uses only exact integer additions.

Set G=22,000. The exact finite calculations give

```
L(G) = 4215,
Σ U(ceil(G/p)) = 4202,
```

where the sum is over the primes 17≤p≤1008.

In any interval I of G integers, at least 4215 candidates are coprime to W.
For a prime p in the sum, its multiples in I have the form pt for t in an
interval of at most ceil(G/p) integers. Since p is coprime to W, the candidate
pt is coprime to W exactly when t is. Therefore at most U(ceil(G/p)) candidates
are divisible by p. The union bound eliminates at most 4202 candidates.
At least thirteen survive. Each survivor q has no prime divisor at most 1008,
so the cyclic-ring construction supplies TD(1009,q).

This improves the earlier elementary gap estimate of 2,776,620 by more than
two orders of magnitude. The final cutoffs above are controlled by the finite
construction, rather than the modest remaining slack in this sieve.

## Closing the infinite induction

Fix either law, with cutoff C from the table. Set

```
G = 22,000,
T = C + 1008G = C + 22,176,000,
N = 1009T.
```

For E1083, N=270,709,766,999; for E1286, N=4,282,494,612,541.
Both are below the respective checked finite endpoints.

Use strong induction on n≥C. The finite certificate handles n≤N. For n>N,
put a=floor((n−T)/1008)+1. The gap bound supplies TD(1009,q) with
a≤q<a+G. Set r=n−1008q. Direct integer inequalities give

```
C ≤ r < T ≤ q < n.
```

The hole r has a model by the finite certificate. The group q has a model by
induction. Idempotent block models exist at 1008=7·9·16 and 1009. Truncating
one group of TD(1009,q) therefore gives a model of order 1008q+r=n.

This induction is `Spectrum.E1083E1286.EffectiveBounds.tail` in Lean, with only
the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Its explicit
hypotheses are the finite interval and the transversal-design gap; it does not
silently assume the desired infinite tail.
