# E1083 and E1286: common-point constructions

29 September 2026. In addition to formalizing the million-element witnesses
from the strong-design argument, a common-point variant gives substantially
smaller models and new polynomial families:

| Law | New family, for every integer t ≥ 0 | First order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1083 | 119(30t+2)² − 6 | 470 |
| E1286 | 119(30t+2)⁴ − 6 | 1898 |
| E1286 | 224(30t+1)⁴ − 6 | 218 |

All three families consist of orders 2 modulo 3 and 2 modulo 4. Thus they fill
infinitely many orders outside the previous idempotent design families.
They do not determine the entire spectrum or give a cofinite cutoff.

## The common-point construction

Suppose there is a TD(k,q), an idempotent k-element model of the law, and
a (q+1)-element model with an idempotent point z. Fill the groups of the
design with copies of the latter model after adjoining one new common
point, identified with z in every copy. Fill each transversal block with
the k-element model. There are kq+1 points altogether.

Within one group together with the common point, use that group's operation.
For points in different groups, use their unique transversal block. All group
operations agree on the common point's diagonal because z*z=z. For an
identity evaluated in one group, the computation stays in that group. For
inputs in different groups, all intermediate products stay in their block,
provided the block's multiplication arguments never coincide.

Every finite idempotent E1083 or E1286 model has precisely this property:
left translations are surjective by the identity, hence injective; the
possible intermediate coincidences then force the original inputs equal.
The two proofs are `discrete1083` and `discrete1286` in
`Spectrum/Equation1083_1286/Gluing.lean`. The generic common-point construction
is proved in `CommonPoint.lean` in the same directory.

## Small examples

Both laws have an idempotent 7-element model `x*y=2x+6y (mod 7)`.
Both have pointed models at 17: `14x+6y` for E1083 and `11x+6y` for E1286,
with zero as the distinguished point. A field of order 16 gives TD(7,16).
The construction therefore gives **113 = 7·16+1** for both laws.

For E1083 the existing 8-element model also has an idempotent point.
Together with TD(7,7), this gives **50 = 7·7+1**.

E1286 also has a pointed 32-element model on five binary coordinates.
Write the operation as `A*x+B*y`, with the two matrices given in
`BinarySeed.lean`. They are multiplication by t³ and t⁴+t³+t²+t in
F₂[t]/(t⁵+t³+1). The proof only needs the matrix identities

    B*A*(A*A+B) = I,
    A+B*(A*A*B+B) = 0.

Lean checks their 25 entries each and then proves the law symbolically.
This does not require constructing the quotient field or checking all
1024 pairs of vectors. Zero is idempotent. With TD(7,31), common-point
gluing yields **218 = 7·31+1**.

## The polynomial families

Let m=30t+2. E1083 has a pointed model of every square order m², from the
Eisenstein companion operation. E1286 has a pointed model at every fourth
power m⁴, from its quartic companion operation. Products with the pointed
17-element seeds give pointed models at 17m² and 17m⁴, respectively.

For E1083 set q=17m²−1. Since m≡2 modulo 30, q≡7 modulo 30.
For E1286 set q=17m⁴−1; now q≡1 modulo 30. In either case gcd(q,30)=1.

A TD(7,q) exists directly over Z/q: use the six coordinates a+ib for
i=0,...,5 and the seventh coordinate b. Differences of distinct finite
slopes have absolute value at most 5, hence are units modulo q. The two
linear equations for a pair of coordinates consequently have a unique
solution. No primality assumption on q or asymptotic existence theorem
is required.

Common-point gluing gives 7q+1, which is exactly the displayed family.
These orders have a single factor of 2. In particular, the construction
escapes the obstruction to an affine construction on a finite abelian
group: that group's two-primary factor would otherwise supply the
forbidden 2-element model.

For the binary seed, instead set m=30t+1 and q=32m⁴−1. Again q≡1 modulo 30.
The product of the pointed 32-model and the quartic companion model has
q+1 points. The same cyclic design and gluing give 224(30t+1)⁴−6.

## The earlier one-hole construction

The strong blocks at 1008=7·9·16 and 1009, together with the nonidempotent
11-element seeds, give order **1017083** for both laws. More generally,
the same proof supplies

    1008·1009^(t+1)+11, for every t ≥ 0.

The models at 1009 have affine operation `958x+52y`. Repeated products give
the full-group models; finite fields give TD(1009,1009^(t+1)). Truncate the
last group to 11 points and apply the gluing theorem. These witnesses are
proved in `DesignWitnesses.lean` without expanding their multiplication tables.

The common-point polynomial families use `CommonPointWitnesses.lean`.
The binary variant is in `BinarySeed.lean`. `PrimeSeeds.lean` additionally
checks 25 prime-field recipes from the earlier survey, using just their two
scalar coefficients. The catalogue exposes the new families as actual
proved lower bounds, with their finite seeds and source declarations.
The complete cofiniteness reduction, including CRT, is checked in
`Equation1083_1286/Cofiniteness.lean`. The shared design-existence theorem
`Spectrum.PBD.wilson_7_9_16` is now proved in `PBD/WilsonInstances.lean`.
Both unconditional cofiniteness results are therefore `PROVED`, with no
numerical cutoff extracted; the individual construction families remain
independently `PROVED`. See [the design formalization](wilson_design_formalization_20260929.md).
