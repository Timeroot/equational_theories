# E1076 has no model of order twelve

The theorem `Spectrum.not_order_1076_12` in
[`OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1076/OrderTwelve/Exclusion.lean)
excludes order twelve. The proof uses **nine compact certificates totaling
5,114,400 bytes (4.88 MiB)**, including the coverage check. Each certificate
checks in **4–24 seconds** in a normal Lean build with dependencies cached;
the largest is 818,568 bytes. The measured maximum process memory was about
2.2 GiB. No `sorry` is used. This replaces the earlier 12.6 MiB candidate bundle
whose replay cost made it unsuitable for the project.

## Mathematical reductions

E1076 reads `y*((x*(x*y))*y)=x`. Finite models are idempotent quasigroups. A left
translation has its distinguished element as its unique fixed point, and
cannot have a nontrivial two-cycle: if `x*(x*y)=y`, substitution into E1076 and
idempotence immediately yield `x=y`. Thus its remaining eleven points have
one of six cycle types: `3+3+5`, `3+4+4`, `3+8`, `4+7`, `5+6`, or `11`.
Consecutive cycle labels give thirteen possible ordered rows, and explicit
conjugating permutations reduce these to six representatives. A small,
independently checked coverage refutation verifies that the rows exhaust the
normalized encoding.

There are two further reductions. First, after fixing row zero we can still
rotate its other cycles, or interchange cycles of equal length. Keeping zero
and one fixed sends `1*0` to one of a short list of representatives. For example,
in either the `3+4+4` or `3+8` case it suffices to allow `1*0` in `{2,3,4}`.
The permutation tables and their inverse, fixed-point, and conjugacy properties
are checked by ordinary kernel reduction in
[`PointData.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1076/OrderTwelve/PointData.lean).
Second, once the first three cycle types have been excluded, **every left
translation of an order-twelve model has no nontrivial three-cycle**. To prove
this for an arbitrary point, move that point to zero and normalize its row;
a three-cycle would place the model in one of the already excluded cases.
This supplies short extra clauses in the remaining searches. The consequence
is specific to order twelve: over `Fin 13`, the affine operation `4x+9y` satisfies
E1076 and its left translations do have three-cycles.

The encoding also records useful consequences of cancellation. The operation
`q(x,y)=x*(x*y)` is itself Latin: its rows square the original left translations,
and E1076 recovers `x` from `q(x,y)` and `y`, giving injectivity in the other
argument. For `x≠y` one also has `y*(x*y)≠x`. These facts, idempotence, and the
conditional three-cycle clauses all have direct Lean soundness proofs in
[`ShortCycles.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1076/ShortCycles.lean)
and [`PointEncoding.lean`](../equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1076/OrderTwelve/PointEncoding.lean).

## Reproduction

```sh
python3 scripts/spectrum_1076_twelve_certificate.py
lake build equational_theories.Spectrum.Equation1076.OrderTwelve.Exclusion
lake env lean --run equational_theories/Spectrum/Equation1076/OrderTwelve/Export.lean /tmp/e1076-twelve-cnf
python3 scripts/spectrum_1076_twelve_certificate.py --check-lean-cnf /tmp/e1076-twelve-cnf
```

All nine independently generated Python CNFs match the Lean exports byte for
byte. The audit also checks certificate hashes, sizes, case specifications,
and generated Lean modules. `--solve` regenerates the certificates, with
bounded solver time and proof-file size; `--only C1G234`, for example, selects
one case. Normal builds never call a model finder. The manifest is
[`1076_order12.json`](../data/spectrum/1076_order12.json).

Earlier unrestricted, idempotent, regular-automorphism, and Mace4 searches are
retained as research history in
[`e1076_positive_search_20261011.json`](../data/spectrum/e1076_positive_search_20261011.json).
Their timeouts play no role in the proof.
