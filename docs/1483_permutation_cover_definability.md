# E1483 permutation covers: a finite FO-definability theorem

30 September 2026. **The full-cover theorem is proved in Lean without admissions.**
The subalgebra extension at the end still has a complete pen-and-paper argument
awaiting formalization. The general finite E1483 → E1485 definability question
remains open.

## Statement

Let \((G,*)\) be a nonempty finite E1483 magma with a constant row
\(0*x=1\), and let \(S\) be a nonempty finite set. Consider any permutation
cover

\[
 (x,u,v)\cdot(y,s,t)
   =\bigl(x*y,B_{xy}^{-1}(v),A_{xy}(s)\bigr)                 \tag{1}
\]

satisfying the E1483 coefficient conditions

\[
 B_{y*x,\,x*(y*z)}=A_{yx},\qquad
 A_{y*x,\,x*(y*z)}=B_{x,\,y*z}.                            \tag{2}
\]

Then **the cover admits a parameter-free first-order definable E1485 operation**
on its carrier. As in the project's finite definability convention, the formula
may depend on the finite source magma. No uniform formula over all finite
carriers is asserted here.

This generalizes the previously formalized companion for one particular
32-element cover. It does not assert mutual FO-definability: the companion may
have more automorphisms than its source.

The proof has three parts: recover the base fibers intrinsically, make every
source automorphism act diagonally in suitable coordinates, and transfer an
E1485 operation preserved by that action.

## 1. The base fibers form a characteristic congruence

The completed constant-row theory supplies:

* The constant row is unique.
* \(x*0=1\), \(1*0=1\), and \(1*1=0\).
* The maps \(x\mapsto1*x\) and \(x\mapsto x*1\) are inverse bijections.

These are already Lean results in `Equation1483/ConstantBasic.lean` and the
preceding constant-row files.

Put \(q=|S|>0\). In the cover, the points with minimum row rank are **exactly**

\[
                             M=\{0\}\times S\times S.     \tag{3}
\]

Indeed, (2) with x equal to 0 gives

\[
                         B_{0,\,y*z}=A_{1,1}.
\]

Every element of an E1483 magma is a product, so \(B_{0,t}\) is independent of
\(t\). A row over 0 therefore has exactly q outputs: its base is always 1,
its first fiber coordinate is fixed, and its second runs through all of S.
For \(x\ne0\), uniqueness of the constant row implies that row x in the base
has at least two different outputs. Each contributes at least q outputs to a
row of the cover, in disjoint base fibers. Its row rank is at least \(2q>q\).

Let \(\lambda\) be the kernel of the projection to G. It is exactly the
congruence generated by \(M\times M\). One inclusion is immediate because
\(\lambda\) contains all pairs in M. For the other, let \(\mu\) be any congruence
identifying every pair of points in M.

Products of two points of M run through the entire fiber over 1, by (1) and
the bijectivity of A and B. Thus \(\mu\) identifies all points over 1 as well.
Varying a point over 1 on the left of a fixed product varies the first fiber
coordinate arbitrarily while keeping the second fixed. Since left translation
by 1 on the base is surjective, this works in every base fiber; choosing the
fixed right input appropriately realizes every value of the second coordinate.
Similarly, varying a point over 1 on the right, and using surjectivity of right
translation by 1, identifies all changes of the second coordinate with the
first fixed. Two such changes connect any pair in a base fiber. Hence
\(\lambda\subseteq\mu\), proving the claim.

Every automorphism preserves row ranks, hence preserves M and the congruence it
generates. Therefore \(\lambda\) is characteristic, and every cover automorphism
induces an automorphism \(\sigma\) of G. It fixes 0 and 1, since their defining
constant-row property is unique.

## 2. Source automorphisms split into coordinate permutations

If \(X_1=(x,u,v')\) and \(X_2=(x,u',v)\), the E1483 word gives the splice identity

\[
                   (Y\cdot X_1)\cdot(X_2\cdot(Y\cdot Z))=(x,u,v)            \tag{4}
\]

for arbitrary Y and Z. The value is independent of those choices: the first
inner factor sees u but not v', and the second sees v but not u', so (4) is
just E1483 evaluated on \((x,u,v)\).

An automorphism preserves the base fibers and this splice. Consequently its
restriction to a fiber is a product of coordinate permutations:

\[
                F(x,u,v)=(\sigma x,U_x(u),V_x(v)).          \tag{5}
\]

For completeness, (4) says that the first component of F(x,u,v) is independent
of v, and the second is independent of u. Bijectivity on the fiber then makes
each coordinate map a bijection.

Preservation of (1) gives the intertwining equations

\[
\begin{aligned}
 U_{x*y}B_{xy}^{-1}&=B_{\sigma x,\sigma y}^{-1}V_x,\\
 V_{x*y}A_{xy}&=A_{\sigma x,\sigma y}U_y.                   \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]

Here concatenation denotes composition, with the rightmost map applied first.
Identify both coordinate sets over every x with the first coordinate set over
1, using

\[
             \alpha_x=B_{1,0}^{-1}A_{0,x},\qquad
             \beta_x=B_{x,0}^{-1}.                         \tag{7}
\]

All maps in (7) are bijections. Because \(0*x=x*0=1\) and \(1*0=1\), equations
(6) specialize to

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \beta_{\sigma x}V_x&=U_1\beta_x,\\
 A_{0,\sigma x}U_x&=V_1A_{0,x},\\
 B_{1,0}^{-1}V_1&=U_1B_{1,0}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last two imply \(\alpha_{\sigma x}U_x=U_1\alpha_x\). Thus under the single
bijection

\[
                 \Theta(x,u,v)=(x,\alpha_x(u),\beta_x(v)),
\]

every source automorphism has the form

\[
                           (x,u,v)\longmapsto(\sigma x,\tau u,\tau v),     \tag{8}
\]

where \(\tau=U_1\). The same permutation acts on both fiber coordinates and
is independent of x. No choice of a point of S has been made.

## 3. An invariant E1485 companion

On G put \(N(x)=1*x\) and \(\phi=N^2\). The existing constant-row theorem says
that \(\phi\) is an automorphism with \(\phi^3=\mathrm{id}\), and that

\[
                           C(x,y)=\phi^2(x)*\phi(y)
\]

satisfies E1485. Every automorphism \(\sigma\) of the base fixes 1, hence commutes
with N and \(\phi\), and therefore preserves C.

On the coordinates supplied by \(\Theta\), take the direct product operation

\[
                       (x,u,v)\mathbin{\square}(y,s,t)=(C(x,y),v,s).
\]

It satisfies E1485 and is preserved by every map (8). Transport it back through
\(\Theta\). Its graph is invariant under every automorphism of the original
finite cover. Automorphism invariance on a finite structure implies
parameter-free first-order definability, giving the claimed companion.
The exact bridge is already formalized as `Magma.definable_of_aut_invariant`.

The Lean proof is in `Spectrum/Equation1483/PermutationDefinability/`:

* `Basic.lean`: `zero_of_small_row` identifies the zero fiber by row rank;
  `map_zero` shows automorphisms preserve it.
* `Fibers.lean`: `fiber_constant` proves preservation of every base fiber,
  and `map_split` separates the two coordinate actions.
* `Companion.lean`: `coordinates_map` proves the common diagonal action;
  `companion_lawful` and `companion_definable` give the E1485 operation and its
  parameter-free FO definition.

The formal proof of fiber preservation uses the same multiplication argument
as §1 directly on a homomorphism, avoiding an explicit congruence construction.
It then only needs coordinate functions, without separately proving they are
bijective. The final theorem's complete axiom list is `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; it has no `sorryAx` or native-check axiom.

Reproduce with:

```sh
lake build equational_theories.Spectrum.Equation1483.PermutationDefinability.Companion
```

## Subalgebras and the eight-point family

The result also applies to any finite subalgebra H whose projected base K has
a constant row. The [rectangular-fiber theorem](1483_subalgebra_research_20260930.md)
gives equally sized coordinate sets in every fiber. Closure inclusions for the
coefficient permutations are then equalities, so choosing bijections with one
common finite set presents H itself as a permutation cover of K. Its E1483
law gives (2) for the resulting coordinate permutations by varying the two
surviving coordinates independently.

Every nonempty subalgebra of the cubic Boolean eight-point base has a constant
row. Hence **every finite subalgebra of every permutation cover of that base
admits an FO-definable E1485 companion**. This is a subclass theorem, not a new
positive entry for the general E1483 → E1485 board cell. No Lean admission or
new completed-board claim is introduced. This subalgebra extension, unlike the
full-cover theorem above, still awaits Lean formalization.
