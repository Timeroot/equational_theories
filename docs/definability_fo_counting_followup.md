# Follow-up: compactness counts with prescribed symmetries

Date: 2026-09-21. **Status: pen-and-paper argument, not formalized in Lean.**
None of the proposed consequences below is included as a new proved fact in the
[completed counting pass](definability_fo_compactness_counting.md).

## Extending the compactness bound

The finite-cover proof should give the same constant K after restricting the
source and target tables to a class preserved by parameter-free FO definitions.
In particular, fix any collection H of permutations of a finite carrier and
count only tables for which every permutation in H is an automorphism. A
forward FO definition preserves these automorphisms, and a fixed recovery
formula still recovers at most one source table from a target table.

Thus the proposed extension is

```text
B.StructuralFrom A  ⇒  ∃ K, ∀ nonempty finite G, ∀ H,
  #H-invariant A-tables on G ≤ K · #H-invariant B-tables on G.
```

The same K must work for every G and H: it comes from the finite collection of
formula pairs covering all A-models. The existing `FORecoveryCompactness` proof
can be adapted by restricting its table subtypes and retaining forward
invariance. The automorphism machinery in `FiniteSymmetry.lean` and
`InvariantFamilyCounting.lean` may help with the applications.

## Independent swaps of pairs

Take `G = Fin m × Fin 2`, for m ≥ 1, and let H be generated by independently
swapping the two elements in each pair. Write s(x) for the partner of x.

An H-invariant table has two binary choices on each pair: its value at `(x,x)`
is x or s(x), and its value at `(x,s(x))` is x or s(x). Swapping the pair
determines the other two entries. On an ordered pair of distinct blocks, there
are four choices, represented by x, s(x), y, and s(y). Independent swaps of all
other blocks exclude outputs outside these four points. Hence there are
`4^(m(m−1))` freely chosen cross-block patterns.

Every unary term stays in the starting pair. If a unary identity admits c of
the four local tables, its invariant-table count is therefore

```text
c^m · 4^(m(m−1)).
```

The local counts are obtained by checking the four choices of diagonal bit d
and partner bit p in `f(x,y) = x XOR (d if x=y else p)`:

| Local count c | Equations |
|---:|---|
| 2 | E3, E47, E614, E817, E4380 |
| 3 | E8, E99, E1020, E1223, E1426, E3456 |
| 4 | E1629 |

For example, E8 allows both partner choices with an identity diagonal, and one
partner choice with a swapping diagonal. E4380 cannot have a swapping diagonal:
it would require the two off-diagonal outputs to agree, while the swap exchanges
both possible outputs. Thus its two allowed local tables are idempotent.

Once both the restricted compactness theorem and these counts are formalized,
any direction from a larger c to a smaller c in this table will be refuted for
unrestricted FO structure. In particular, E8 → E47, E614, E817, and E4380 are
promising next targets. A ratio such as `(3/2)^m` is unbounded even though no
individual finite carrier is a counting counterexample to a model-dependent
choice of FO formulas. **This method would not refute finite FO structure.**

The immediate implementation tasks are a compactness theorem for definability-
closed table families, a complete parameterization of the pair-swap-invariant
tables, and Lean proofs of the local counts. The numerical check of four local
tables is evidence for the argument, not a substitute for those proofs.
