Relations and evidence

What an arrow means

For every source magma satisfying A, the relation A → B asks for a B-operation on the same carrier. The defining formula or term may depend on that magma.

Relation Requirement
Implication The original operation already satisfies B.
Term structural A binary term defines the new operation; another term recovers the original.
FO structural Parameter-free first-order formulas define each operation from the other.
Term definable A binary term defines the new operation.
FO definable A parameter-free first-order formula defines the new operation.

Each has an all-magmas variant and a finite-only variant. Implication entails term structural; term structural entails both FO structural and term definable; either of those entails FO definable. An all-magmas result restricts to finite magmas. Refutations propagate in the opposite direction.

Proofs, claims, and unknowns

Proved cells are Lean results or consequences obtained by reflexivity, transitivity, duality, the relation hierarchy, and contraposition. The evidence viewer reconstructs source-labelled paths and counterexample certificates. Derived cells are not presented as individually named Lean theorems.

Conjectural cells use a purported argument without a completed Lean proof, including reported ATP results. A candidate formula with no purported proof is unknown. A theorem containing or depending on a pending proof is never promoted to proved merely because it has a Lean declaration.

For spectra, each exact formula, lower bound, upper bound, and cofiniteness claim has its own evidence. The build checks catalogue formulas and transitive dependencies. Native computation proofs retain Lean's native-computation trust boundary.

Equivalence classes

Only proved arrows in both directions merge classes. Distinct displayed classes may still merge until at least one direction between them has a proved refutation. The possible-merges view lists precisely those unsettled class pairs, including pairs with a conjectural separation.

Graphiti draws the order between proved classes. It omits arrows implied by other paths among the visible nodes. Selecting an arrow reconstructs its Lean provenance; selecting a class exposes members and evidence for both directions.
